The 13-story tower at Piotrkowska 165/169 is the lesser-known half of the small complex that most people just call “Central.” The department store next door got all the attention, but the office block was built at the same time, in 1972, for the Społem cooperative, and for years it housed the cooperative’s Łódź administration. It was designed by Maciej Gintowt along with Ewa and Maciej Krasiński, the same team behind the Supersam in Warsaw and Spodek in Katowice, and the whole complex was selected through a SARP competition back in 1961. The tower is 45 meters tall and stands as a fairly pure piece of brutalism—washed concrete panels, repetitive horizontal window bands, a raised first floor on pillars that creates a covered passage at ground level.

While the department store kept operating and was even renovated inside in 2019, the office tower was emptied out and left to decay. It was put up for sale in 2018. A developer eventually bought it and commissioned a conversion project from architect Marcin Tomaszewski, which would have turned the upper floors into around 40 apartments, added a two-level entrance hall and service units on the ground floor, and put a gym and massage room in the basement. Building permits were secured, but the developer backed out. The building went back on the market in 2025, now with a price tag around 20 million złoty and the completed design included in the package. In the meantime, urban explorers have been inside and reported that the lower floors still hold remnants of furniture and equipment from the Społem days, while most of the upper floors have been stripped down to bare concrete. The building is not going to be demolished—heritage protections and the local zoning plan require the original structure to be kept—so whatever happens, the concrete frame is staying. It is currently still waiting for a buyer willing to actually finish the job.
