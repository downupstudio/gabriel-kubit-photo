Łódź developed as a textile center from the 1820s, when authorities designated it a factory settlement. Its population grew from 767 in 1820 to over 314,000 by the end of the 19th century, driven by migration from rural areas and abroad. By 1900, textiles accounted for 94.1% of the city's production, with cotton dominating until the 1880s, when wool's share rose to 37.1%. Large enterprises replaced small workshops: by 1913, 35 factories employed over 500 workers each. The industry collapsed after 1989; the Poltex cotton combine, which had employed over 9,000 people, filed for bankruptcy in 1991.
