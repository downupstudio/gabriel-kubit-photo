Nowe Rokicie is an area of Łódź designated in 2005 for the Municipal Information System (SIM), not a formal administrative estate but a wayfinding zone. It lies in the Górna district and forms the north-eastern part of the Rokicie administrative estate. It was a village before incorporation, with parts annexed to Łódź in 1906 and 1915, and definitively in 1946.

Its boundaries run along Wróblewskiego Street to the north, where it meets the SIM area of Politechniczna in Polesie; Politechniki Avenue to the east, bordering Górniak; Pabianicka Street to the south, meeting Kurak; and to the west it adjoins the Rokicie and Nowe Sady SIM areas. The Jasień river flows through the area.

Before the war, the area was largely undeveloped, with brickworks and factory premises scattered among open land. The turning point came with the construction of a large public school building, designed by city architect Wacław Kowalewski, whose main works were completed in 1926. It still stands at Przyszkole Street and houses Primary School No. 42. The complex was built in the National Style, referencing Stanislavian classicism, and is entered in the register of monuments.

Large-scale housing construction began after World War II. Planning for the new estate started around 1953 with technical and economic guidelines. The layout concentrated commercial and cultural functions along a north–south axis, and the estate was intended as a model of a sunny, lively social housing district under difficult post-war conditions. The first blocks were four-storey buildings in a more economical form, and the estate was populated largely by residents displaced from demolished inner-city buildings, as well as families of ministry, militia, and military employees.

Three buildings stand out in the estate's architecture. The first is the "łamaniec" (roughly, "the zigzag"), a mixed residential and commercial building in the centre of the estate designed by engineer Edmund Orlik around 1957. Its name comes from its floor plan, where the walls meet at angles other than right angles, giving the elevations a zigzagging appearance. The ground floor has a sculptural treatment of the commercial space with a glazed display window projecting in front of the façade, and the building runs along one of the estate's two main commercial routes. The second is a health centre with a pharmacy, designed around 1960 by Jerzy Jakubczak, a simple modernist building with a functionally resolved interior and an imposing circular staircase in the main hall, referencing the latest international tendencies of the time.

The third and largest architectural event was the complex of four 13-storey towers completed around 1962, which became the estate's signature and the first complex of its kind in Łódź. They were designed at the Office of Studies and Projects for Industrial Construction in Warsaw under architect Włodzimierz Minich, on the site of five point blocks originally planned by Orlik. The towers have very simple plans and structures, well-proportioned elevations, and were built using an advanced technology for the time that allowed such tall buildings to be erected without heavy equipment. By the early 1970s, industrial building technology was used for the final blocks of the estate.

The estate has lost the flowery character it had in its early years. In recent years, the area has seen some infrastructure work, including a planned comprehensive renovation of two key streets, Piękna and Cieszkowskiego, with 370 new parking spaces, new water mains, pavements, asphalt, and trees. A developer has also proposed a new housing estate for around 2,000 people on Holownicza Street near the Jasień river, consisting of ten blocks.
