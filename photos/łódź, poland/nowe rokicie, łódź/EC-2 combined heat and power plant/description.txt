EC2 is the former Łódź heat and power plant at Wróblewskiego 26, a 20-hectare complex that operated from the mid-1950s until 2015 and is now being demolished to make way for a housing estate. EC2 is largely coming down — though a handful of its buildings are listed as monuments, and the main hall is meant to survive.

The plant was built as Elektrociepłownia im. Włodzimierza Lenina, designed by engineer Mariusz Sowiński. Construction began in June 1954, the first boiler and turbine started up in 1957, and the full complex was finished in 1961. It was the first modern combined heat and power plant in Łódź after the war, supplying electricity, system heat to the Górna districts including Retkinia and Karolew, and process steam to nearby industry. The architecture is socialist realist, with the main building, a 300-metre machine hall, two cooling towers, and three chimneys—two at 120 metres and one at 160 metres—dominating the skyline. The site also contained air-raid shelters for several dozen people. Its industrial interiors and chimney stacks appeared in film and television, including the series Stawka większa niż życie and the music video for Taconafide’s “Tamagotchi”.

The plant was shut down on 31 March 2015 after 57 years of operation, with Veolia citing worn-out equipment, falling demand for heat and electricity, and stricter environmental rules coming into force in 2016. After closure, the site changed hands several times. A first attempt at demolition in 2019 was halted by the provincial monuments conservator because it was carried out without the required permit. The current owner, JHM Development, bought the shares of the bankrupt company Hakamore in 2021 and now holds perpetual usufruct rights to the entire 20-hectare site.

Demolition properly began around Christmas 2025, starting with the gatehouse and gate. The two cooling towers and three chimneys were also scheduled for removal, with JHM arguing that technical surveys showed advanced degradation, no realistic prospect of repair, and a risk to the surroundings. None of those elements—the towers or the chimneys—were in the monuments register, and the conservator had agreed that they should be struck from the heritage inventory. What is protected is a group of buildings: the main power plant building with the control room’s technical equipment, the administration building, the gatehouse with its drive-through gate, the social and management building, and the land itself. The perimeter fence is also in the heritage inventory.

The three chimneys — two at 120 metres and one at 160 metres—were brought down on 20 October 2026. The operation was handled by JHM Development using the explosive method: the chimneys were demolished in their above-ground and underground sections, foundations included, in a controlled blast. Preparations had been underway for weeks, with an inventory of neighbouring buildings taken before and after the works to document their condition in case of claims. Residents in the area, particularly those living opposite on Wróblewskiego and in the Nowa Przędzalnia estate, had been worried about the noise and dust, and the investor kept the exact timing quiet for safety reasons, notifying only those directly affected. Because the chimneys were never in the monuments register—the conservator had explicitly declined to list them back in 2019—no heritage permit was needed for their removal.

What remains of EC2 now is the protected group of buildings: the main power plant hall, the administration building, the gatehouse, the social and management building, and the perimeter fence. The future of the site is still not fully settled. JHM has shown visualisations of a mixed-use development—rows of multi-family housing, a shopping centre, passageways, the restored main power plant building, and the cooling towers kept as landmarks—but the company says these images, prepared for the Expo Real fair in Munich, are not binding. The current plan is described as “undefined”; JHM is talking to the city and looking for a shared concept, with concrete plans to be specified within a year or two. The scale being discussed is around 2,500 flats, plus commercial, office, and leisure space, some of it inside the listed buildings in a mixed-use formula. A new public street is also planned, linking Aleja Politechniki with Wieniawskiego, partly along the route of the old railway siding under the Jana Pawła II viaduct. Architects and local groups have argued that the main EC2 building should keep its industrial identity and take on a cultural function rather than being swallowed by housing, and residents have said the area already has too many blocks and would be better served by a park or entertainment space. For now, the chimneys are gone, the main hall is protected, and the rest of the complex is gradually disappearing.
