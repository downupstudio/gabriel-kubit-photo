EC-2 was the first of three thermal power plants built in Łódź after World War II, constructed between 1955 and 1960 at Wróblewskiego street. It began producing heat and electricity on 1 February 1957. The plant initially had eight coal-fired boilers and six turbo-generators, and it was later expanded with two mazut-fired boilers. Its electrical capacity reached 87 MW and thermal capacity 578 MWt. The facility supplied electricity to southern districts of Łódź and Pabianice, and heat primarily to the Retkinia, Karolew, and Czerwony Rynek housing estates. The plant ceased operation on 31 March 2015 after 58 years, due to declining heat demand, worn-out equipment, and new environmental regulations. It was sold by Veolia in 2017 to the Hakamore company, which began demolition. Parts of the complex, including the main building, administrative building, and gatehouse, were entered into the register of monuments, which halted full demolition. The site is now planned for residential development, though preservationists argue the main building should retain its industrial character and be adapted for cultural use. The three flue-gas chimneys at EC-2 — two at 120 metres and one at 160 metres — were unfortunately demolished as part of the site's redevelopment.
