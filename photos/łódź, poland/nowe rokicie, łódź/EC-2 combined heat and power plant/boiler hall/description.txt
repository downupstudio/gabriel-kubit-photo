The boiler hall at EC-2 is one of the most massive and intact spaces left in the entire complex. Originally, this hall housed nine boilers, but four of them were dismantled before the plant was shut down, leaving only boilers 5 through 9 in place. Of those remaining five, four were OP-130 units (numbered 5, 6, 7, and 8) and the fifth was a larger OP-140 (boiler number 9). All of them were fed with pulverized coal, each generating between 130 and 140 tons of steam per hour.

The demolition work has hit this hall hard, but not completely. The upper floor was demolished, and boiler K-5 was partially dismantled along with it. Boilers K-6, K-7, and K-8 survived in good condition, though the metal components have been cut out for scrap, leaving the massive steel skeletons and pressure parts behind. Boiler K-9 was dismantled from the top down, so it now looks as if it has been sliced in half.

Despite the damage and the scrapping, the boiler hall remains one of the best-preserved parts of the entire EC-2 site. The scale of the space, the huge steel frames, and the remaining pressure vessels still give a strong sense of how the plant operated.
