The turbine hall at EC-2 is a vast, 300-metre-long space that originally housed six turbosets, though only three remained operational by the time the plant closed. These three steam turbines had a combined electrical output of 87 MW, taking the high-pressure steam piped over from the boiler house and converting it into power and heat. The structure itself was built for heavy industry, with massive steel columns weighing 92 tons each supporting roof girders that spanned 27 metres.

The scrapping of the site hit this hall brutally. Every turbine and generator was cut up for scrap, and the floor that once held the turbosets was demolished entirely. 

Despite being stripped of its machinery, the turbine hall is still protected as part of the main building complex. The massive overhead cranes remain suspended in the rafters, and the long stretches of industrial glazing still light up the empty shell. It stands as a hollow, echoing space, but the sheer scale of the room still gives a clear picture of the heavy machinery that used to run here.
