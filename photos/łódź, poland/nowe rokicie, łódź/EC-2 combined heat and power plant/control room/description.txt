The control room at EC-2 is the only interior in the plant still listed in the heritage register—the main building was entered into the register together with its technical equipment, which makes this room the single most legally protected space inside the complex. Operators worked there for decades, monitoring the boilers and turbines and making the decisions that kept the city supplied with power and heat. The room itself was the most representative interior of the whole plant, built with a glazed roof and filled with computers and historic control devices for managing electricity production.

The room had hundreds of buttons, indicators and levers, a dense wall of instrumentation that recorded and regulated everything happening in the boiler and turbine halls. In 2023 its walls were still largely intact. Since then the wall panels have been cut out for scrap, and the damage has not stopped at the panelling. The provincial monuments conservator inspected the site and concluded that the control room had been almost completely destroyed, and the case was reported to the prosecutor’s office in Łódź-Górna on suspicion of destroying protected technical equipment.

Only the main control desk survives, and it is in poor condition. The control room remains one of the best records of how EC-2 operated and also one of the most heavily damaged parts of the complex.
